Lens options, simply explained

Different lenses for
different needs.

Your prescription and daily routine help determine which lens may suit you. An eye-care professional or optician can explain your options.

01

Single vision

One main correction across the lens, often for distance or near vision.

02

Bifocal

Two viewing zones in one lens, typically for distance and near tasks.

03

Progressive

A gradual change in lens power across near, intermediate and distance zones.

04

Coatings & use

Anti-reflective, UV and computer-use options depend on the product and availability.

Lens designs and coatings are general categories, not a confirmed product list for this clinic. Call to check availability

Small signs are worth noticing

Eye health, for
every chapter.

Vision can change with age, health and daily habits. These notes can help you know what to watch for and when to ask a qualified eye-care professional.

01

Children & students

Squinting, rubbing the eyes, headaches after reading, or difficulty seeing the classroom board may be reasons to arrange a professional eye assessment.

Children’s vision signs ↗
02

Diabetes & vision

Diabetes can affect the eyes, and some eye disease may not cause early symptoms. Ask your healthcare professional how often you should have an eye examination.

Diabetes and eye exams ↗
03

Screens & eye comfort

Long screen sessions can leave your eyes feeling tired or dry. Take regular breaks, blink often and adjust your screen and lighting. Seek advice if discomfort persists.

Ask us a question ↗
04

Vision through the years

Changes such as difficulty reading up close, seeing at night or adjusting to glare deserve attention. A qualified professional can advise what kind of assessment is appropriate.

A quick eye-health glossary

Common vision changes

These are general descriptions, not a diagnosis.

MyopiaDistance vision may look blurry.
HyperopiaClose-up focus may be difficult.
AstigmatismVision may appear blurred or distorted.
PresbyopiaNear focus can become harder with age.
Dry eyeMay cause a gritty, irritated or dry feeling.
Cataract & glaucomaNeed professional assessment; treatment availability here must be confirmed.
!

Sudden vision loss, serious eye injury, chemical exposure, severe eye pain, or a sudden shower of floaters with flashes or a dark shadow needs urgent medical attention. Do not wait for a routine optical appointment. Learn about sudden floaters and flashes ↗
